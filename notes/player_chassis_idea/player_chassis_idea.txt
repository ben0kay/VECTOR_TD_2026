# VECTOR TD — PLAYER CHASSIS / CLASSES

Player chooses a chassis at the beginning of a level.

Chassis should create clearly different gameplay roles without becoming an overly complicated RPG class system.

Persistent player upgrades and local level upgrades can further specialize each chassis.

Main idea: **the chassis determines what the player is naturally good at, while upgrades determine how they develop it.**

---

## ASSAULT

Direct combat-focused chassis.

Designed for players who want to personally contribute heavily to killing enemies and intercepting dangerous groups before they reach the base.

**Strengths:**
- High weapon damage
- Good fire rate / weapon performance
- Good movement speed
- Good general combat capability

**Weaknesses:**
- Poor repair ability
- Poor building/support capability
- Average durability

**Gameplay:**
Spend most of the level fighting enemies, hunting priority targets, intercepting siege units, and supporting towers with direct firepower.

Relies more heavily on repair towers/drones and automated base infrastructure for maintenance.

---

## HEAVY

Frontline combat/tank chassis.

Designed for players who want to physically hold dangerous defensive positions and survive large enemy attacks.

**Strengths:**
- Very high hull / HP
- High armour
- Strong shields
- Good combat capability

**Weaknesses:**
- Slow movement
- Slow acceleration
- Poor repair capability
- Cannot respond across the map as quickly as Assault

**Gameplay:**
Move to the most dangerous defensive front and stay there.

Excellent for fighting large groups at close range, protecting damaged defenses, and surviving situations that would force other chassis to retreat.

Relies heavily on automated repair infrastructure.

---

## ENGINEER

Base maintenance and infrastructure chassis.

Designed for players who enjoy personally maintaining, repairing, mining, and supporting their base rather than maximizing direct combat power.

**Strengths:**
- Very fast repairs
- Better building interaction
- Good mining capability
- Good mobility
- Strong infrastructure utility

**Weaknesses:**
- Lower weapon damage
- Lower combat durability
- Poorer direct combat than Assault or Heavy

**Gameplay:**
Move around the base repairing walls, towers and infrastructure while helping wherever necessary.

Can still fight enemies, but its main advantage comes from keeping the player's defenses operational.

An Engineer player can rely less heavily on dedicated repair towers because they can personally perform much of that role.

---

## SUPPORT

Battlefield support / tower assistance chassis.

Unlike Engineer, which directly repairs and maintains buildings, Support improves the performance of operational defenses.

**Strengths:**
- Balanced combat ability
- Balanced durability
- Strong tower support
- Good battlefield utility

**Weaknesses:**
- Less direct damage than Assault
- Less durability than Heavy
- Worse repairs than Engineer

**Possible defining feature:**

**COMMAND LINK**

Towers within a radius around the Support player receive small bonuses such as improved fire rate, targeting, range, or energy efficiency.

Keep the bonus relatively simple rather than creating many complicated abilities.

**Gameplay:**
Move between defensive fronts depending on where the largest attack is occurring.

The player's location becomes strategically important because defenses perform better while Support is nearby.

---

## POSSIBLE LATER CLASS — SCOUT

Not necessary for the initial implementation.

Fast reconnaissance/exploration chassis that could become more useful as radar, scanning, procedural exploration, and resource discovery systems expand.

**Strengths:**
- Very high movement speed
- Very high acceleration
- Improved radar/scanner
- Good exploration capability

**Weaknesses:**
- Low HP
- Weak direct combat
- Poor repairs

**Gameplay:**
Explore the map, locate resources, reveal incoming threats, hunt enemy scouts, and rapidly respond to distant problems.

---

# INITIAL CLASS SET

Recommended initial implementation:

**ASSAULT**
> I kill things.

**HEAVY**
> I hold the line.

**ENGINEER**
> I keep the base alive.

**SUPPORT**
> I make the defenses stronger.

Possible later addition:

**SCOUT**
> I control mobility and information.

---

# DESIGN RULE

Avoid creating many slightly different chassis such as Heavy / Tank / Armoured / Juggernaut.

Each chassis should exist because it produces a noticeably different way of playing the level.

The major trade-off should be **player time and responsibility**, not just stats.

For example:

**ASSAULT / HEAVY**
Player spends more time fighting.
Base needs more automated repair/support infrastructure.

**ENGINEER**
Player personally handles much more maintenance.
Can spend less on automated repair infrastructure.

**SUPPORT**
Player moves toward important defensive fronts to strengthen tower performance.

This allows base design and player chassis choice to affect each other.

---

# UPGRADES

Chassis provides the baseline identity.

Persistent upgrades provide long-term progression.

Local level upgrades provide temporary specialization during an individual level.

Example:

Two players can both select ASSAULT but develop completely differently through upgrades.

One could specialize into:
- High fire rate
- Movement
- Energy weapons

Another could specialize into:
- Heavy damage
- Shields
- Explosives

They remain ASSAULT chassis, but their upgrade choices create different builds.

The chassis should therefore provide a **strong starting identity without determining the player's entire build.**
